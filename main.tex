\section{Introduction}

The ability to run highly resolved, submesoscale-permitting global simulations of ocean dynamics is a computationally intensive process that has limited examples of successful applications \citep{uchida2022cloud,epke2025overturning}. These small-scale physical dynamics are known to be an important component of surface layer turbulence \citep{dong2024submesoscales} and improving our understanding of the transport of heat, salt, momentum, and tracers between the surface and deep ocean \citep{qu.ea:22, hilditch2025observations}. Previous attempts to simulate small-scale coastal ocean features using the Department of Energy's global unstructured ocean model (MPAS-O, Modeling Predictions Across Scales - Ocean) found that there were numerous grid-related challenges that impacted the limitations of model refinement capabilities, in addition to the severe computational demand  (Schlichting et al., in review). Capturing feedback processes of these features on global scale changes is thus limited to nesting finer-scale regional models within global simulations.

 Regionally refined meshes face additional challenges related to the choice of vertical resolution and mixing parameterizations that are designed for generic open ocean conditions and may be inadequate for high-resolution or coastal simulations. Conservation of mass and momentum being the main one? Common issues encountered when coupling on rectangular meshes. To the best of the authors' knowledge, this effort may also mark one of the first instances wherein an unstructured global mesh has been used in an offline coupling to provide initial and boundary conditions for a structured grid mesh.

This work implements a proof-of-concept adaptive approach to implement one-way offline nesting between an unstructured parent ocean model MPAS-O and a structured child model ROMS in three regional domains of varied complexity. Previous work has demonstrated how both ocean models similarly represent physical dynamics as a consequence of baroclinic instabilities in an idealized domain from 10 km to 100 m (Hinson et al., in review), as well as the limitations of regionally focused refinement in MPAS-O within a specified coastal domain (Schlichting et al., in review). This paper will outline the methodology by which the global MPAS-O solution can be remapped onto a regional ROMS grid for use as initial and boundary conditions. The results of physical dynamics within four regional domains will then also be shown.

\subsection{MPAS-O and ROMS model domains}
MPAS-O is a global ocean model that uses a horizontally varying unstructured mesh and a z* vertical coordinate system \citep{ringler2013multi}. For this work, a global simulation run at an average horizontal resolution of approxiately 10 km was applied, with specific parameterizations that include...

Four ROMS \citep{shchepetkin2005regional} modeling domains were used for these experiments, all with approximately spanning a range of 600 m to 4 km horizontal spatial resolution (Fig.~\ref{fig:zed_comparison}). The first domain in the North Atlantic is an implementation of the NISKINE model (Simmons reference), sited to capture key physical dynamics associated with the initiation of the Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation (AMOC) between 56 to 60 $^\circ$N and 26 to 20 $^\circ$W. This North Atlantic domain has an average horizontal spatial resolution of 2 km with no land mask and an average depth of approximately 2750 m and represents the simplest configuration of an open ocean simulation with relatively consistent bathymetry. The Gulf Stream domain represents an increased step in complexity, with more sharply varying bathymetry in an open ocean environment and an energetic western boundary current. The domain utilizes a curvilinear grid developed using pygridgen (citation?) designed to follow the approximate range of the meandering western boundary current as it departs from the U.S. East Coast between 33 to 43 $^\circ$N and 75 to 57 $^\circ$W. This model has an average horizontal spatial resolution of 1.6 km with no land mask, but its sharper bathymetry gradients are due to its inclusion of the southern edge of Georges Bank. Third, an implementation of a previously developed model covering most of the Bay of Bengal (Simmons reference) is also applied to test the capabilities of this research in a nearshore environment with a land mask between 9 to 24 $^\circ$N and 80 to 97 $^\circ$E. This model has an average horizontal resolution of 4 km with masking on all sides of the domain, steep bathymetry gradients, and receives river inputs from a global reanalysis product \citep{harrigan2020glofas}. Lastly, we utilize another previously developed ROMS application within the Chesapeake Bay (ROMS-ECB, \cite{st2024sensitivity}) to test the remapping methodology in a setting with large tracer gradients, a highly complex land-masked coastline, and riverine inputs. This estuarine model, which extends between 36.5 to 39.6 $^\circ$N and 77.3 to 75 $^\circ$W, has an average horizontal resolution of 600 m and 20 vertical levels to better capture physical and biogeochemical complexities associated with a deep channel and the large contributions of riverine discharge to mixing processes.

\subsection{Domain analyses and methodological modifications}

Time series of average surface kinetic energy levels were computed for each of the regional realistic domains as this metric provides a relatively simple check on a combination of remapped velocity fields. This comparison relied on the native grid MPAS-O solution (where kinetic energy is directly computed online at the cell center), the remapped solution, and the regionally refined solution where the remapped solution is used to provide initial and boundary conditions. For both the remapped and regionally refined solutions, surface kinetic energy is computed during post-processing by averaging the velocity components from cell edges to cell centers \citep{xroms}.

Multiple points of failure in applying remapped initial and boundary fields to ROMS domains became apparent as additional challenges of regional domains were introduced. In particular, the steep continental slope bathymetric gradients found in the Bay of Bengal and the comparatively coarse resolution of the Chesapeake Bay represented in MPAS-O led to multiple model failures. Within the remapped solution in the Bay of Bengal, insufficient smoothing of vertical levels led to the erroneous production of anomalously warm water at mid-depths ($>$ 28$^\circ$C at approximately 100-200 m) at multiple points near the masked coastline. This subtle but persistent error was then corrected by applying vertical smoothing to scalar fields, and produced a more stable simulation. Within the Chesapeake Bay domain, the sizable number of grid cells that were incorrectly masked by MPAS-O made it immediately apparent that a direct application of the remapped solution was insufficient due to its coarse resolution. In this instance, a blend of the two grids and remapped fields was performed to better ensure stability in the ROMS simulation. Within the estuary, delineated by the Bay mouth, all scalar, velocity, and bathymetry fields were set to pre-determined conditions that best matched MPAS-O remapped estimates for a particular date. In an area between the Bay mouth and the offshore edge of the model domain, a linearly decreasing (increasing) mixture of the ROMS (MPAS-O) fields were averaged together until they reached a point further offshore where the entirety of the remapped solution was supplied by MPAS-O alone. The only component of this simulation that remained unchanged by MPAS-O contributions was the land mask, which if used would introduce additional extrapolations and would likely be insufficient for ROMS model stability.
