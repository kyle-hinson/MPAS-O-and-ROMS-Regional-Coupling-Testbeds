\begin{abstract}
Global ocean models must compromise on horizontal resolution, even when regional refinement is employed, in order to afford climate-length integrations within reasonable wall-clock times. Regional downscaling provides a practical approach for embedding finer process resolution within global simulations, but requires the consistent transfer of three-dimensional state fields between coupled model components. Differences in mesh topology, the horizontal resolutions represented by the parent and regional domains, and the vertical coordinate systems used by the models introduce additional complications in constructing a coupler whose accuracy and conservation properties can be verified. We present a separable \MPAS$\leftrightarrow$\ROMS tensor-product transfer that applies a globally conservative horizontal map, level by level, over the common mesh support, followed by a monotone, integral-preserving vertical remap for salinity, temperature, and velocity fields supplied as multiscale boundary conditions. Verification with a smooth analytic field and daily-mean ocean state shows decreasing errors as target-grid sequences are refined and verifies the consistency and component conservation properties of the transfer algorithm. Additionally, such convergence studies also help compute the optimal regional model grid resolutions that balance computational cost and accuracy. We further investigate feature resolution in the deep-ocean North Atlantic and Gulf Stream domains, together with coastal and estuarine applications in the Bay of Bengal and Chesapeake Bay. These regional applications develop finer-scale variability and enhanced submesoscale structure across open-ocean, coastal, and estuarine settings, demonstrating the feasibility of the proposed multiscale downscaling approach.
\end{abstract}

\subsection{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
The transfer improves over the coarse portion of the tested sequences, but its composite round-trip error saturates for real fields on the finest pair. For the controlled analytic field, isolating the individual stages shows that refining the target alone runs into the limit set by the fixed parent representation, while cancellation between the forward and reverse legs makes the round trip a different quantity from the one-way forcing error. Requiring the vertical interpolant to conserve layer integrals makes the composed tensor-product transfer first order overall, which is consistent with the measured decrease, though confirming the rate by fitting would require a longer sequence than the four grids used here.

The regional applications show finer-scale relative-vorticity structure and domain-dependent kinetic-energy changes across open-ocean, boundary-current, coastal, and estuarine settings. These descriptive results combine transferred information with variability generated by regional dynamics and should not substitute for transfer verification. 
%The application ratios are 0.21 (North Atlantic), 0.24 (Gulf Stream), 0.35 (Bay of Bengal), and 0.06 (Chesapeake Bay); the Gulf Stream and Bay of Bengal grids coincide with the r3 verification grids.

Several limitations qualify these results. The parent resolution is fixed and varies in space, the evaluation support changes with target refinement, and the Chesapeake blending procedure is not specified in enough detail to be repeated exactly. Velocities are transferred componentwise and remain Earth-relative and cell centered, without rotation to the target curvilinear basis, interpolation to staggered points, or any constraint on the discrete divergence. Bathymetry smoothing stabilized the Bay of Bengal case but its parameters are domain dependent. Neither finer targets nor higher-order interpolation can remove the errors imposed by the parent representation, the boundary forcing, the land masks, or estuarine structure that the parent never resolved.

\section{Summary and future directions}\label{sec:conclusions}
We presented a separable volumetric transfer between unlike horizontal meshes and vertical coordinates, combining normalized horizontal remapping with column-wise monotone, reconstructed-profile integral-preserving vertical remapping. Verification with analytic and real fields showed errors decreasing over the coarse part of the sequence and saturating for real fields on the finest pair, while controlled analytic experiments distinguished the one-way forcing error from the round trip. Because the vertical interpolant is constrained to conserve layer integrals, the composed scheme is first order overall, and the observed decrease is consistent with that expectation, although four grids are too few to fit the rate; isolating the column operator on synthetic vertical domains would measure it directly. Higher order is available from quasi-interpolants, at the cost of conservation, monotonicity, or both.

The regional applications developed finer-scale vorticity structure and domain-dependent changes in kinetic energy. These cases suggest a target scale near $\hRatio\approx0.2$ as a reasonable starting point, though we would recommend confirming it with a short refinement sequence in each new domain. The Bay of Bengal required bathymetry smoothing before it would run stably, and Chesapeake Bay required native geometry together with blended estuarine fields. Both cases illustrate why the fidelity of the transfer and the physical interpretation of the regional solution need to be assessed separately.

Two checks would strengthen the verification most directly. A common-support comparison for the real fields on the finest grid pair would resolve whether the apparent saturation is genuine, and exercising the column remapper alone on synthetic vertical domains, where the target range is embedded in, coincides with, or extends beyond the source range, would establish the order of the vertical operator without the confounding effects of the horizontal map and the changing support. Beyond those, forward-only tests for real tracers against surrogate references, sensitivity studies spanning several parent resolutions, and repeated timings would all help. With those checks in hand, the transfer can be evaluated for online one-way exchange and, eventually, for two-way coupling, where explicit tracer-inventory and land--estuary consistency tests become essential.
