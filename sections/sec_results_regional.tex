
Running \ROMS on finer regional meshes with \MPAS-derived boundary conditions produces finer-scale spatial variability across the regional domains (Fig.~\ref{fig:zeta_snapshots}). The kinetic-energy records change relative to the \MPAS and directly remapped states in a domain-dependent way (Fig.~\ref{fig:region_KE}), reflecting both the transferred state and the variability generated by the regional dynamics. In all regions the original MPAS-O solution's surface KE (black lines) is greater than the remapped surface KE (blue lines), which represents the starting point for the ROMS solution. Following an initial condition shock in the ROMS solutions, the surface KE time series then follows a period of more rapid oscillations over the course of a day that decreases over the length of the simulation period. The ROMS KE time series tends to follow the pattern of the MPAS and remapped solutions for open-ocean domains (Fig.~\ref{fig:region_KE}a-b), but then notably diverges in more complex coastal domain settings (Fig.~\ref{fig:region_KE}c-d), particularly in the Chesapeake Bay where the coarseness of the global MPAS-O grid is more inherently limiting.

All regional domains typically employ a nudging layer of 5-10 cells that ensure boundary stability. The interpretation of regional model results relies on snapshots of surface normalized relative vorticity ($\zeta/f$) and surface kinetic-energy (KE) time series. The snapshots of $\zeta/f$ show points of (in)stability between the boundary layer due to numerical gradients and are plotted at the halfway point (30 days) of the simulation period. Area-weighted surface KE time series are computed equivalently for the remapped and ROMS solutions, and are clipped to the fractional \MPAS cells that lie within the extent of the ROMS grid before also being computed as an area-weighted average.


The figures in this subsection show vorticity filaments and fronts at scales the \MPAS mesh does not represent. These snapshots establish that the structures are present in the regional simulations and absent from \MPAS. They do not show where the structures originate, how they evolve, or whether they propagate. Those questions require a scale decomposition or time-resolved diagnostic that is not presented here.
